# MASH Antifragile: Locked Specification

Oct 18, 2026 · @Neil Schwab

[image: MASH Antifragile title card]

## Core thesis

MASH teaches the nervous system what a safe, connected, purposeful life feels like, so a person comes to want that more than the drug. Addiction is a response to an environment; change the environment and you change what the person reaches for.

Most programmes discharge people after about 30 days, while post-acute withdrawal (PAWS) runs 6 to 24 months. MASH fills that gap with three months in a land-based community where belonging, mastery, rhythm and physical work are part of every day, not a weekly session. Every newcomer spends their first month shadowing a senior in that senior's final month, so the culture is handed down person to person.

The approach stands on established practice:

- **Community Reinforcement Approach** (Hunt and Azrin; later Meyers): make a sober life more rewarding than using. Its family version, CRAFT, matches the MASH family layer.
- **Contingency management**: tangible rewards for recovery behaviour, among the best-supported addiction interventions, especially for stimulants. The MASH points and games system is a form of it.
- **Environment research**: Alexander's "Rat Park" studies (around 1980) found isolated animals chose morphine far more than animals in an enriched social setting. Later replications were mixed, so it illustrates the idea rather than proving it.

The D.O.S.E. model (dopamine, oxytocin, serotonin, endorphins) is the framework's explanation of why connection, mastery, rhythm and movement work. It is an explanatory model, not a measured result.

MASH works alongside medical treatment, never instead of it. Medicine stabilizes the body; MASH changes the need.

## Who MASH is for

MASH serves adults whose lives are cycling through emergency rooms, police contact and shelters, because that is where both the human need and the public cost are concentrated.

**Eligibility (proposed).**

1. Age 18 or older
2. Homeless, unstably housed or cycling through institutions for 12 months or more
3. Two or more in the past year: emergency department visits, police or court contact, shelter use, or a failed discharge from treatment
4. Medically stable enough for a non-clinical camp, or stabilized first through the medical partner
5. Chooses to come: the Choice Point presents Path A and Path B honestly, and Path B is never assigned

[image: The Choice Point: intake to Path A or Path B, with pause and return allowed at every stage]

*The Choice Point: non-coercive, matched to capacity, and safe to return to.*

The screen matters to the savings case as well as to safety. The Housing First trial found the largest returns among the highest service users: $21.72 saved per $10 for the top 10% [At Home/Chez Soi]. The Conservative Floor in Section 8 is valid only for this group.

**Not in scope for the pilot.** People under 18; anyone needing hospital-level medical or psychiatric care; anyone ordered to attend as a court condition. People on parole or probation may still choose to come. A youth stream and court-ordered entry each need their own legal review before they are added.

## The camp

One camp holds 200 residents in 20 nodes, with rolling intake so it stays full all year. Stays are three months, and returning participants are housed inside the same 200 beds.

*Concept rendering, not a real site photo.*

| Element | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Core capacity | 200 residents in 20 tents |
| Node | 8 participants + 2 Controllers (peer mentors with lived experience) |
| Stay | 3 months (about 90 days); the newcomer's first month overlaps a senior's final month |
| Shadow pairing | Every newcomer is paired with one senior for their first month |
| Intake | Rolling, node by node: as a node graduates, a new node arrives |
| Throughput | About 600 people a year, plus about 525 short return stays |

| Proposed location | Crown land near Cobalt / Coleman Township, or a donated or municipal site |
| Tents | Surplus Canadian Armed Forces tents, if released |

**Site criteria (from Enter the MASH).** 40+ acres of flat or rolling ground with a slope for the mudslide. **No open water on site** (no river, lake or pond, to prevent drowning) and no cliffs. A well or piped water. Within 1 to 2 hours of a hospital and RAAM clinic; for Cobalt, that's Temiskaming Shores. Land held by the co-op or in trust so it can't be sold out from under the community.

**Infrastructure.**

- 20 military modular tents (10 people each), rated to −40°C, with propane heaters, **CO detectors**, solar lighting, cots, sleeping bags and lockable footlockers
- Mess tent with propane stoves and communal seating
- Triage and harm reduction tent (cot, lockbox, naloxone, medical supplies) and a separate User Area
- Central fire pit, never allowed to go cold during operating months
- Shower tent (propane-heated), outhouses built by residents, a well with hand pump and solar pump
- Activity area: heavy bag, drum logs, cleared ground for dancing and bingo, mudslide
- Optional chapel tent: non-denominational, quiet space

**Why returns fit inside the camp.** 200 beds × 365 days = 73,000 bed-days. 600 three-month stays use 54,000 and the 525 return stays a year (Section 8) use about 15,750, a total of 69,750, or about 96% full. No separate return node is needed, but there is little slack: if returns run higher than the halving cascade, the camp needs more beds or fewer first stays.

**Controllers in Year 1.** Controllers are meant to be graduates, but the first cohort has none. Year 1 needs hired peer support workers with lived experience, paid from the Operations stream. **Budget warning:** Operations is $2.25M a year at the 3-month stay. Forty peer workers at around $45,000 each would take about $1.8M of it, leaving little for the medical float, insurance and site. Year 1 may need one hired Controller per node (20) plus a graduate Controller from the first cohort, or Controller wages counted in Community & Elders. Settle this before the budget goes out (open decision 4).

**Harm reduction and triage (as set out in Enter the MASH).** Three spaces sit at the edge of the camp, each with a clear path back to triage:

- **Triage Centre.** Intake, RAFT assessment, medical screening and the 90-minute LLJ stabilization protocol.
- **Harm Reduction Centre.** PAWS symptom checks, naloxone training, wound care, safe consumption education and warm handoffs to RAAM.
- **User Area.** A separate, supervised space so no one uses alone, with sharps disposal and naloxone on the wall.

These spaces are peer-run by Controllers and graduates trained in first aid, naloxone and overdose response, with Neil as harm reduction lead. A mobile float team from the medical partner (CAMH or RAAM) provides the clinical cover and carries its own clinical insurance.

**The User Area needs a legal route before it opens.** Supervised consumption in Canada needs a federal exemption from Health Canada under section 56.1 of the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act. Ontario's 2024 Community Care and Recovery Act also restricts consumption sites, and the province has been moving funding to HART hubs, which don't offer supervised use. This is open decision 8: apply for the exemption, operate the User Area under the medical partner's licence, or open without a User Area and add it once it's approved. *Check current Ontario status before submitting.*

## The pathway

A participant moves through three months, then graduates node by node into paid work at GSRT Earthworks. Marcus's Day 88 discharge review in the case file falls at the end of this window.

1. **Day 1: immediate entry.** A bed, food, gear, a node and a shadow senior the same day, with no waitlist. Triage within 2 hours: RAFT check (roof, addiction medical need, fundamentals, target) and the 90-minute stabilization protocol.
2. **Month 1 (Weeks 1–4): stabilize and shadow.** Medical stabilization through the partner where needed. The newcomer lives alongside their senior, who models the rhythm and the tools. Learning to self-regulate by watching someone a few weeks further along.
3. **Month 2 (Weeks 5–8): skills and roles.** Responsibility, peer structure and points earned through practice, contribution and the camp games.
4. **Month 3 (Weeks 9–13): senior and mentor.** The participant now shadows a newcomer of their own, which earns seniority and steadies their own recovery. DEEDSPROOF mode unlocks in the app, and the node graduates together at about Day 90.

**Daily rhythm.**

| Time | Activity | Layer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sunrise | One-Breath Reset; HALT check with buddy | Presence |
| Morning huddle | Beacon Huddle: 1–10 light-check, "one pattern I noticed" | Gratitude, Acceptance |
| Morning work | Lean-to building, well maintenance, land stewardship, trail clearing | Dopamine, endorphins |
| Midday meal | Communal cooking; one gratitude spoken before eating | Oxytocin, serotonin |
| Afternoon | Boxing, drumming, dancing, bingo, skills training, G.A.P.S. Interruption Book study | All four |
| Evening meal | Rotating cooking crews; tribe tables | Oxytocin |
| Night (Slow Soak) | Campfire, talking stone, Fish Bowl release, Sacred Pause, Tier 1–3 curriculum, talisman ceremonies | All four |
| Wind-down | One-line journal: "What did today teach me?" | Surrender |

Every arrival is assessed in the Triage Centre within 2 hours, using RAFT and the 90-minute G.A.P.S. Between You and Me (LLJ) stabilization protocol.

**Graduation readiness.** Points earned, W-score at or near the Day 90 target of 54, Secure Connection at least Developing (Cs 1.0), a written 12 Blocks relapse plan, Controller endorsement, and the person's own choice.

**Graduation: the DEEDSPROOF badge and the Bonding Party.** At the end of each cycle the camp holds the Bonding Party. The new arrivals honour the outgoing crew, each graduate's Controller pins their **DEEDSPROOF** badge (TRUST · STABILIZE · BEGIN), and the MASH Covenant is offered, never forced. A graduate can speak it before the fire or receive the badge in silence; both are honoured.

**The exit package.**

1. **The Anchor Kit:** study guide, practice cards and journal
2. **The Keeper's Manual:** their personal operating system for the next year
3. **The Pallet:** a skills and growth tracker
4. **The Badge:** DEEDSPROOF
5. **The camp phone number:** the Prodigal Son hotline, always answered

**The Four-Book Journey (lifelong, voluntary).**

| Stage | When | Badge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEEDSPROOF | First exit, after 3 months | Trust · Stabilize · Begin |
| FISHING HOLE | Second exit, Year 2 (deep immersive) | Dive Deep · Emerge Strong |

**Fix the DEEDSPROOF module before anyone uses it for work matching.**

1. **Drop the WHMIS labels.** WHMIS is Canada's hazardous-products system and has no colour categories called White, Blue or Black, and nobody can "submit" a Green one. The app shows "Current WHMIS category" on curriculum colours, which a safety inspector or funder will catch immediately. Keep WHMIS where it belongs: mandatory safety training at every level (the Safety Precedence card already says this). The near-identical names WHIMS and WHMIS make the confusion worse, so consider renaming the colour wheel (for example, the CCGBI colour wheel).
2. **Take childhood history out of the work Match Score.** The app promises the childhood section affects "pacing and support, never your eligibility", but its Shade score carries 30% of the Match Score. Use Shade for pacing and support only, and keep it out of job matching.
3. **GOLD at 55+ in job matching is an Ontario Human Rights Code risk.** Age is a protected ground in employment. Either treat Elder standing as an honour separate from job allocation, or set it up as a formal special program under the Code (section 14) with legal advice.
4. **Scoring bugs.** In Wheel 1 a person can click several answers per question and every click adds a point, and scores carry over between sessions. In Wheel 3 any answer of 4 or 5 raises Tint, including agreeing with the stress (neuroticism) items, so people who report more distress score *higher*. There's no reverse scoring.
5. **Label the instruments honestly.** The personality items are custom, not a validated Big Five scale. Four of the five childhood questions aren't from the standard ACE questionnaire. Call both "adapted, not validated".
6. **Privacy on shared camp devices.** Everything, including childhood answers, is stored unencrypted in the browser on one device. That's fine for a demo, but not on a shared camp tablet.

**Wages.** The MASH stay itself is not waged: food, housing, clothing and gear are provided. Paid work begins at Earthworks.

## G.A.P.S. firmware: what the camp actually runs

The premise: the drug isn't the core problem, the environment is. The camp gives the nervous system a felt experience of secure connection, so the person has a natural source of what the drug was standing in for. The neurochemical mapping below is an **explanatory model** (the D.O.S.E. framing). It is not a clinical mechanism claim and isn't presented to funders as one.

| Capacity | Primary signal (model) | Secondary | Camp practice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gratitude** | Serotonin | Dopamine | Naming one gratitude at every meal; the evening gratitude round |
| **Acceptance** | Oxytocin | — | Fish Bowl circle; node belonging; family sessions |
| **Presence** | Endorphins | — | Sacred Pause; movement, boxing, drumming; Lighthouse Stance |
| **Surrender** | Serotonin | Oxytocin | Asking for help aloud; the Prodigal Son return without shame |

**Tools taught at camp.**

- **Sacred Pause.** Stop, breathe and name the feeling before acting.
- **The 20-minute craving window.** Cravings typically peak and pass; ride it out with the node instead of alone.
- **Fish Bowl.** One person speaks in the centre; the circle listens without fixing.
- **Lighthouse Stance.** Grounded posture and steady presence, used by Controllers to de-escalate.

Also taught: 4-in, 6-out breathing ×3; 5-4-3-2-1 grounding; the Rule of One; the talking stone; and the Fish Bowl phrase "There's [x] swimming in the bowl."

**The Universal Interruption Protocol.** When a participant hits a Block: Name it → Ground the body → Make contact → Apply the capacity → Wait 20 minutes → Document it.

**The 12 Blocks mapped to G.A.P.S.**

| Capacity | Blocks it interrupts |
| --- | --- |
| Gratitude | 1 The Drift · 7 The Pit |
| Acceptance | 2 The Mask · 6 The Fire · 8 The Bargain |
| Presence | 3 The Build · 5 The Fog · 9 The Tunnel · 11 The Edge |
| Surrender | 4 The Wall *(called "The Freeze" in the government proposal)* · 12 The Fall |

*Two gaps to close:* **Block 10 has no name in any of the five documents**, and Block 4 has two names. Pick one name each before the Interruption Book or app is finalised.

**Motivational Interviewing alignment** (Miller & Rollnick, 2023): Partnership → Gratitude, Acceptance → Acceptance, Compassion → Presence, Empowerment → Surrender.

**Mapping conflict to settle.** Enter the MASH and the government proposal describe Gratitude as "serotonin + oxytocin", and describe Acceptance, Presence and Surrender as functions (observer stance, Sacred Pause, parasympathetic shift) rather than single chemicals. The table above uses the locked mapping from the Alexia paper. Use one version everywhere: update the older documents to the locked mapping, or change the lock.

**Relapse prevention: 12 Blocks and seven PAWS hurdles.** Relapse-prevention planning is adapted from Gorski's CENAPS model. The seven post-acute withdrawal (PAWS) hurdles used at camp are **adapted from Gorski (2013)**; Gorski's original lists six symptom groups, so the camp version must always be cited as an adaptation.

**Gamification and points.** Points are earned for showing up, completing roles, helping others and practising the tools. Points decide graduation order. The evidence anchor is **contingency management**, which has strong trial support for stimulant and other substance use. The camp games (including the Daily Dose family game) are the delivery format, not the evidence.

**Family layer.** Families and Elders are brought in on purpose: In Deeper Waters sessions, grandparents as anchors, and Elder mentors. The evidence anchors are the **Community Reinforcement Approach (CRA)** and **CRAFT**, which teach families to reinforce sober behaviour and have outperformed confrontational and Al-Anon-style approaches at getting a loved one into treatment.

**Why it's built this way (Rat Park).** Alexander's Rat Park experiments are the inspiration for "change the environment, not just the person". Replications have been mixed, so it is cited as the inspiration, never as proof.

## Safeguards: MASH works alongside medicine, never instead

Physical dependence is real and some withdrawals kill. The camp is a recovery environment, not a detox unit, so a medical partnership is a condition of opening, not an add-on.

- **Alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal can be fatal** (seizures, delirium tremens). Anyone at risk is withdrawn under medical management before or on arrival, through a mobile medical team or a RAAM (Rapid Access Addiction Medicine) partner. *Partner proposed, not yet confirmed.*
- **Opioid agonist treatment (OAT) is welcomed.** Methadone and buprenorphine are evidence-based and reduce deaths. Nobody is asked to stop OAT to enter. Opioids act on the endorphin (opioid) receptors; the camp's natural endorphin practices sit beside OAT, not in place of it.
- **Naloxone at every node.** Every Controller is trained and every tent has a kit.
- **Overdose risk on exit and return.** Tolerance drops during a stay, so a relapse after MASH is the highest-risk moment. Every person gets an overdose-risk briefing and naloxone at exit, and a saved place for returns, so a relapse leads back in, not to a morgue.
- **Crisis lines.** 9-8-8 (suicide crisis) and 9-1-1 are posted and drilled.
- **Mental health.** Controllers are peer support workers, not clinicians. Psychiatric need is referred to the medical partner.
- **Prodigal Son Protocol.** Anyone who leaves can return without shame for a 30-day return stay inside the 200 beds.
- **Consent and data.** All outcome data is collected with consent. Where First Nations are involved, data governance follows **OCAP®** (ownership, control, access, possession).
- **Voluntary.** No one is placed at MASH by a court. A person on parole or probation may choose to come.

**Incidents: what stays in camp and what doesn't.** The earlier documents say every incident is resolved internally, with "no police, no court, no charges". No funder, insurer or medical partner will sign that. The safe version keeps your restorative approach for most incidents and draws a clear line:

- **Resolved in camp** (Fish Bowl, repair, Controller mentoring): mischief, vandalism, arguments, minor scuffles, rule breaks, and keys taken before a vehicle moves.
- **Always outside help:** any serious injury (ambulance), overdose not reversed on site, sexual violence, weapons, credible threats, or assault where the person harmed wants to report. **The person harmed decides whether police are called, not the camp.**
- **Merrick (Merit) Ratio.** The 1 negative to 40 positive moments ratio is a target Controllers log, not a measured result. Present it that way.

**Liability and insurance.** Ontario's Good Samaritan Act protects people giving emergency first aid without expecting to be paid, so it probably doesn't cover paid Controllers in their job. The operator needs its own general liability and abuse insurance, and the $50,000 insurance and admin line in the old budget won't come close. The federal Good Samaritan Drug Overdose Act still matters: it protects residents from simple possession charges when they call 9-1-1 for an overdose.

**Health privacy.** The older documents cite PIPEDA. Health records in Ontario usually fall under PHIPA, especially once a medical partner is involved, and OCAP® applies to First Nations data. Confirm with a privacy lawyer before the app goes live.

## Measurement: what gets counted and how

**The W-score.** Each person starts at the intake baseline of **W = 11.2**, a quarter of the burned-out values in the occupational health and safety literature. W is a wellbeing index, not dollars, and it is never added to or multiplied with costs. The illustrative Marcus case runs from 11.2 at intake to 48.0 at Day 88 (illustrative simulation, not observed data). Secure Connection (Cs) is locked to 0.8, 1.0 or 1.2.

**What a funder will see.**

| Measure | Source | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W-score and Cs | Camp assessment | Intake, Day 30, 60, 90, exit |
| Perceived Stress Scale (PSS) | Validated instrument | Intake and exit |
| Recovery capital (suggest BARC-10) | Validated instrument | Intake and exit |
| Retention, completion and returns | Camp records | Ongoing |
| ED visits and police/court contacts | Linked records, with consent | 6 and 12 months after exit |
| Housing and employment | Follow-up | 6 and 12 months after exit |
| Overdoses and deaths | Medical partner and coroner data | Ongoing |

**Not yet validated.** The SAF and OCI scales are camp-designed and must be labelled as unvalidated until tested. ProQOL and BAT measure worker burnout and are used for Controllers only, never for participants.

**Honesty rule.** All dollar figures in this spec are a simulation built on conservative published floors. They become evidence only once the pilot's own measured outcomes replace them.

## The seed: what it costs and what it has to save

*Every figure here is checked against the GSRT Mathematics Manual (corrected v2) for formulas and the Citations & Evidence Addendum for sources. Track named on every line; tracks never blended.*

**Stay cost.** $80 a day for food, housing, hygiene and upkeep, plus $60 a month for clothing.

- 90 days × $80 = $7,200, plus 3 × $60 = $180, which gives $7,380. Rounded up, **$7,500 per 3-month stay.**
- 600 people a year × $7,500 = **$4.5M** in direct participant costs.

**The MASH waterfall.** MASH runs on its own engine, the same Justice Floor split as Earthworks (Mathematics Manual, Section 6: 20/40/20/10/10 and R = P ÷ 0.40). Participant costs are the 40% stream, so gross = $4.5M ÷ 0.40.

| Stream | Share | Annual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participants (food, housing, clothing, gear) | 40% | $4,500,000 |
| Operations (Controllers, medical float, site, insurance, transport) | 20% | $2,250,000 |
| Community & Elders | 20% | $2,250,000 |
| Progression (graduation into Earthworks) | 10% | $1,125,000 |
| Reserve (seeds the next camp) | 10% | $1,125,000 |
| **Gross** | **100%** | **$11,250,000** |

**Returns (the halving cascade).** Half of graduates return once, half of those again, half again: 0.5 + 0.25 + 0.125 = **0.875 returns per person**, or 525 returns a year at 30 days each. A returning person comes back as a teacher with lived experience of the relapse cycle, not as a failure.

- 525 × 30 days × $82 a day = **$1.29M** direct
- Grossed up through the waterfall (÷ 0.40): about **+$3.23M**
- Steady-state gross with returns: **about $14.5M a year** ($14,478,750)

**The break-even test.** Gross cost per person is $11.25M ÷ 600 = **$18,750** (about **$24,131** with returns). The question is what share of a person's current public cost MASH has to avoid to pay for itself.

| Public cost per person per year | Track and source | Break-even (first stays) | Break-even (with returns) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $120,000 | Observed | 15.6% | 20.1% |
| **$80,000** | **Conservative (× 2/3)** | **23.4%** | **30.2%** |
| $56,000 | Evidence Floor (Latimer et al. 2017, five-city CMAJ Open study) | 33.5% | 43.1% |

**Why $80,000 holds, and when it doesn't.** The $120,000 composite cites agency figures the addendum could not trace, so it is not the anchor. Two-thirds of the published Calgary chronic-homelessness cost of $134,642 (2008, cited in Gaetz 2012) is $89,761, above the $80,000 floor. That holds only if eligibility (Section 2) actually selects chronic high users; At Home/Chez Soi found the largest savings in exactly that group ($21.72 saved per $10 for the top 10% of service users). Loosen the screen and use the Evidence Floor row.

**The one-line test.** At the Conservative track, MASH pays for itself if it avoids about **a quarter** of what these citizens already cost the public (under a third once returns are counted). At the peer-reviewed Evidence Floor it needs about a third (43% with returns).

**The seed and the next Godspring.** The ask is **$11.25M for Year 1**, rising toward about $14.5M a year once return stays build up. This supersedes the earlier $3.5M figure (which covered roughly one cycle of food and clothing). The 10% reserve, $1.125M a year, funds the next camp. Each site seeds the next, which replaces the unsourced ×10 multiplier (Cascading Seed Model).
